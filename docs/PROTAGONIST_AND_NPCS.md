# DOL Protagonist & NPC Foundation

## Status
Game 1 — Character and social prototype foundation

## Design Principle

The first characters should make Longlat feel inhabited before they make the mythology feel explained.

Historical and cultural research should inform character details, but fictional characters remain fictional. DOL should avoid turning real communities into exposition devices, villains, or costume sets.

## Protagonist — Working Concept

**Name:** TBA  
**Community:** Longlat Marches, fictional local community  
**Role:** Runner / repairer / pathfinder — final role TBD

The protagonist is useful in ordinary life before the Dawn. They know local paths, people, river crossings, weather patterns, and practical problems. They are not trained as a supernatural hero.

### Starting Character Questions

- What responsibility keeps them in the Marches?
- Who depends on them?
- What relationship is damaged or unresolved?
- What practical skill lets the player interact with the environment?
- What do they believe about the strange event before seeing it?
- What do they get wrong?

### Arc

**Ordinary responsibility → personal investigation → first supernatural encounter → responsibility beyond home → growing understanding**

Do not make the protagonist “the chosen one” in the first slice.

## Core NPC Set

### 1. Settlement Elder / Keeper

Function:
- establishes community memory;
- knows the old road;
- provides a grounded interpretation of the disturbance.

Personality:
- observant;
- practical;
- not automatically correct.

Gameplay:
- optional conversation;
- points toward the old road.

### 2. Young Trader / Courier

Function:
- shows that Longlat is connected to other places;
- provides movement and economic life.

Personality:
- energetic;
- curious;
- more willing to speculate than the elder.

Gameplay:
- optional route information;
- environmental clue.

### 3. River Worker

Function:
- makes the river crossing feel lived-in;
- notices changes in water, animals, or traffic.

Gameplay:
- explains why the normal crossing has become unsafe;
- can become the player's first human confirmation that something is wrong.

### 4. Worshipper / Community Member

Function:
- demonstrates that religious life exists as ordinary social life.

The character may belong to a church, mosque, or locally rooted traditional community depending on final settlement research.

They should not be an automatic “mystic NPC.”

### 5. Child / Younger Resident

Function:
- gives the player a non-combatant perspective;
- notices something adults dismiss.

Use sparingly. Do not make children supernatural exposition machines.

### 6. Returning Traveler

Function:
- creates the first credible report of something strange beyond the settlement.

The traveler should disagree with at least one other interpretation. This establishes uncertainty.

## NPC Design Rule

Every recurring NPC should have at least:

- a practical reason to be where they are;
- a relationship to another person;
- a desire;
- a small problem unrelated to the Dawn;
- one piece of information;
- a viewpoint that may be incomplete.

## Dialogue Rule

Prefer:

**observation → opinion → disagreement → player choice**

over:

**NPC → mythology lecture → player nods**

## Cultural Research Gate

Before assigning a real-world cultural identity, record:

1. community/context;
2. language context;
3. historical setting;
4. relevant everyday practices;
5. source basis;
6. variation or disagreement;
7. fictional changes;
8. representation risks.

The character should still function as a person after the cultural reference is removed.

## First Slice NPC Count

Target:

- 5–8 visible NPCs;
- 3–4 interactable NPCs;
- 1–2 recurring characters;
- no unnecessary faction system.

## Success Test

A player should be able to remember at least two NPCs by what they **want or do**, not merely by what mythology they represent.
