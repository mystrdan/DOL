# DOL Cultural Review Protocol

## Purpose

Prevent DOL from turning African cultures, religions, mythologies, languages, or material traditions into a single generic fantasy aesthetic.

Recent game-design research supports participatory and community-aware approaches to African cultural representation rather than treating culture as surface decoration. citeturn0search0turn0search1

## Four Evidence States

### DOCUMENTED
Supported by credible historical, ethnographic, scholarly, museum/archive, or community sources.

### INTERPRETATION
A reasoned interpretation where evidence is incomplete, contested, regional, or variable.

### DOL ORIGINAL
Created for the fictional DOL universe.

### UNVERIFIED
Appears plausible or widely repeated but has not yet met the evidence threshold.

**UNVERIFIED material cannot become a defining gameplay mechanic or final cultural asset.**

## Review Record

Every important real-world reference should record:

- community/tradition;
- region;
- language/context;
- historical period where relevant;
- source;
- variation;
- what is documented;
- what is interpretation;
- what DOL changes;
- why it is being adapted;
- gameplay/story/visual role;
- representation risk;
- review status.

## Sacred Material Rule

If a reference involves:
- deity/divine being;
- ancestor;
- ritual;
- shrine/sacred place;
- religious symbol;
- sacred object;
- initiation;
- divination;
- healing practice;

then it receives additional review before becoming a mechanic, weapon, item, costume, collectible, enemy, or quest objective.

Not every sacred element needs to become gameplay.

## Community Variation Rule

Never assume that one community's version is the universal African version.

Record:
**community → specific tradition → local variation → source → DOL adaptation**

Broad labels such as “African,” “Bantu,” “West African,” “Nilotic,” or “San” are research indexes, not automatically single mythologies.

## Language Rule

Do not invent a fake pan-African language.

For fictional Longlat speech:
- define it as fictional;
- identify real-world inspirations separately;
- avoid presenting reconstructed/invented words as authentic language;
- document names and terminology.

## Visual Rule

A reference image is evidence of an image, not proof of cultural accuracy.

Final assets require provenance and context.

## Religion in the World

Churches, mosques, traditional sacred places, and ordinary community spaces can coexist where the fictional setting supports them.

They are not automatically:
- factions;
- enemy bases;
- loot locations;
- magic shops;
- quest hubs.

The goal is a lived world containing people with different beliefs and interpretations.

## Review Gates

### Gate 1 — Research
Is the source credible?

### Gate 2 — Context
Do we understand who uses/used the element and where?

### Gate 3 — Variation
Are important regional/community differences recorded?

### Gate 4 — Adaptation
What exactly are we changing for DOL?

### Gate 5 — Representation
Could the adaptation create a stereotype, flatten a living tradition, or mislabel a sacred element?

### Gate 6 — Gameplay
Does the adaptation improve the game, or is it present only because it looks “African”?

## Rejection Conditions

Reject or redesign when:
- source cannot be verified;
- a broad stereotype is being used as cultural fact;
- a sacred practice is being converted into a generic spell;
- a community-specific element is presented as continent-wide;
- the design depends on colonial caricature;
- the reference adds aesthetics but no meaningful world/game function.

## Review Philosophy

**Accuracy before spectacle. Context before mechanics. People before mythology.**

DOL can be fictional without pretending its inspirations are fictional.
