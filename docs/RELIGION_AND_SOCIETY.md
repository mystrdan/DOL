# Religion & Society in DOL

## Purpose

Religion exists in DOL as part of everyday society, not as a checklist of fantasy factions.

Real-world inspiration remains distinct from DOL fiction.

## Worldbuilding Rule

Longlat may contain communities with:
- Christian traditions;
- Muslim traditions;
- locally rooted Indigenous/traditional religious traditions;
- people who identify with more than one cultural or religious influence;
- secular or non-observant people.

## In-Game Spaces

Depending on the fictional community, players may encounter:
- churches;
- mosques;
- traditional sacred places;
- shrines or ritual spaces where appropriate to the fictional culture;
- cemeteries and burial grounds;
- sacred groves, rivers, hills, trees, or other landscapes;
- homes and community spaces where religious life is practiced.

These spaces are primarily worldbuilding locations. They do not automatically become combat arenas, loot locations, quest hubs, or supernatural mechanics.

## Representation Rules

1. Do not present Christianity, Islam, or Indigenous traditions as one monolithic system.
2. Do not make one faith the default “good” side and another the “evil” side.
3. Do not use real sacred practices as generic magic spells.
4. Do not use sacred objects as generic loot without research and context.
5. Do not call every traditional sacred place a “juju place.”
6. When a fictional sacred place draws from a specific tradition, record the source community and adaptation decision.
7. Religious characters should have ordinary human motivations beyond religion.
8. Religious spaces can be peaceful, ordinary, busy, contested, abandoned, or affected by the Dawn depending on the story.

## The Dawn

The supernatural crisis may affect religious communities differently.

A church, mosque, and traditional sacred site might all experience the same anomaly while the people around them interpret it through different worldviews.

DOL should not declare which interpretation is universally correct simply to create a fantasy hierarchy.

## Game Rule

Religion adds **life, identity, history, conflict, architecture, NPC behavior, atmosphere, and perspective**.

It does not need to become a separate gameplay system.

**People first. Religion as part of life. Supernatural fiction where the story needs it.**
