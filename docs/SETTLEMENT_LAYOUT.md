# Longlat Marches — Settlement Layout

## Status
Game 1 — First playable settlement foundation

## Purpose

The settlement is the player's first proof that DOL is a living world.

It should communicate ordinary life before supernatural spectacle.

## Spatial Structure

**Gate/Path → Common Yard → Homes → Market Edge → Worship/Gathering Space → Farm/Work Edge → Old Road**

The settlement should be compact enough to understand within minutes.

## 1. Arrival Path

The player enters from the Longlat side.

Visual goals:
- foot traffic;
- carts or carried goods;
- vegetation and cultivated land;
- signs of maintenance;
- distant Longlat silhouette.

No combat here.

## 2. Common Yard

A social center rather than a quest hub.

Possible activity:
- conversation;
- food preparation;
- repairs;
- children playing;
- trading;
- people resting.

The player should be able to pass through without stopping.

## 3. Homes

Do not make every building identical.

Show differences through:
- household size;
- work;
- storage;
- cooking;
- building materials;
- maintenance;
- personal objects.

Do not assign architecture to a real culture merely because it “looks African.”

## 4. Market Edge

Small-scale exchange:
- food;
- tools;
- household goods;
- agricultural products;
- traveler goods.

The market establishes Longlat as part of a network.

No large shop UI is required for the first prototype.

## 5. Worship / Gathering Space

The final form depends on Game 1 research and the fictional community.

Potential spaces:
- church;
- mosque;
- locally rooted traditional sacred place;
- ordinary community gathering area.

Multiple traditions may coexist across Longlat, but the first settlement does not need to contain every one.

Religious architecture is worldbuilding first, not a dungeon category.

## 6. Farm / Work Edge

The settlement transitions into:
- cultivated land;
- animal areas where appropriate;
- storage;
- work paths;
- water access.

This creates a natural route toward the river and Marches.

## 7. Old Road Exit

The old road should feel older than the settlement.

Environmental clues:
- worn route;
- old markers;
- abandoned maintenance;
- vegetation reclaiming sections;
- fewer travelers.

This is where the player's normal world begins to become uncertain.

## Day-One Gameplay

Only a few interactions are required:

- talk;
- inspect;
- move;
- observe;
- follow.

No crafting, economy simulation, farming system, or house interiors are required for the vertical slice.

## Dawn Foreshadowing

The settlement should contain only subtle anomalies:

- an animal refusing a familiar path;
- an unusual silence;
- a person returning early;
- a damaged marker;
- water behaving strangely;
- a distant sound with no obvious source.

Do not reveal the supernatural too early.

## Visual Rule

**Lived-in > exotic.**

The settlement should look like a place built for people, work, family, movement, worship, trade, and weather — not an attraction assembled from “African” visual clichés.
