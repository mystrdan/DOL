# DOL Character Foundation

## Purpose

Define the protagonist without locking the character before the world and Game 1 research are ready.

## Player Character

The protagonist is an original fictional person from the DOL world.

They are not a historical person, deity, or direct recreation of a real-world mythological hero.

## Starting Design

The protagonist begins as an ordinary person with:
- a home/community connection;
- a practical skill;
- personal relationships;
- a reason to investigate the first Dawn disturbance;
- limited knowledge of the supernatural.

They should become important through choices and events, not because the story immediately declares them a chosen one.

## Character Test

The protagonist needs:
- a reason to keep moving;
- something personal to lose;
- something they do not understand;
- a useful ability;
- a flaw or limitation;
- relationships that can change.

## Gameplay Connection

The protagonist's first abilities should emerge from gameplay needs.

Initial prototype abilities:
- movement;
- basic attack;
- defensive action;
- interaction;
- one signature ability.

The signature ability is DOL-original unless a researched tradition has explicitly passed the adaptation gate.

## Cultural Identity

The protagonist can belong to a fictional Longlat community inspired by a specific real-world cultural context.

Before locking that identity, document:
1. source inspiration;
2. historical/contextual research;
3. what is fictionalized;
4. what is avoided;
5. how the identity affects the character beyond clothing.

Culture should influence relationships, language choices, worldview, architecture, food, social expectations, names, and everyday behavior—not become a costume.

## Design Goal

The player should first think:

**“I want to know what happens to this person.”**

Not:

**“This character exists to explain African mythology.”**
