# Game 1 Research Priority

## Status
Research planning — active

## Principle

DOL needs broad continental awareness and deep local research, but Game 1 cannot wait for an impossible encyclopedia of every African tradition.

Research must support concrete design decisions.

## Priority A — Longlat's Immediate Cultural Context

Deep research is required for the real-world contexts that inspire:

- settlement life;
- architecture;
- clothing/material culture;
- food/agriculture;
- trade;
- naming/language;
- social relationships;
- religious diversity;
- landscape.

No single “African aesthetic” should be used as a substitute.

## Priority B — Game 1 Mythological Influences

Select only a small number of traditions for deep adaptation in the first game.

For each selected tradition record:

1. community;
2. region;
3. language context;
4. historical setting;
5. cosmology;
6. supernatural beings;
7. oral narratives;
8. sacred places;
9. ritual/social context;
10. variation;
11. evidence;
12. living-community considerations;
13. DOL adaptation proposal;
14. elements explicitly rejected.

## Priority C — Environmental Knowledge

Research the ecological setting of the fictional Marches:

- river systems;
- farming patterns;
- seasonal changes;
- vegetation;
- wildlife;
- travel;
- settlement placement.

The environment should not exist only to make combat arenas.

## Priority D — Religion in Everyday Life

Research how religious identity and practice can intersect with:

- family;
- community;
- work;
- festivals;
- mourning;
- travel;
- food;
- authority;
- healing;
- moral life.

Do not assume religion is a separate “magic system.”

## Priority E — Material Culture

Build a reference library for:

- tools;
- containers;
- textiles;
- building materials;
- transport;
- musical instruments;
- household objects;
- agricultural implements;
- market goods.

Each asset reference should record where it comes from and what has been fictionalized.

## Adaptation Gate

A real-world element may enter gameplay only after:

**Source → Context → Variation → Classification → Adaptation → Review**

Possible classifications:

- DOCUMENTED
- INTERPRETATION
- DOL ORIGINAL
- UNVERIFIED

If evidence is weak, keep it out of the core gameplay system.

## Research Stopping Rule

Stop researching a topic for the current prototype when:

- the relevant context is understood;
- important variation is recorded;
- representation risks are identified;
- the gameplay role is clear;
- further research is unlikely to change the prototype decision.

Then build and test.

## What We Should Not Do Yet

Do not block development waiting for:

- every African mythology;
- a final pantheon;
- a complete fictional language;
- a complete continental history;
- dozens of playable characters;
- hundreds of researched creatures.

Broad coverage continues in parallel, while Game 1 deep research remains focused.

## Research-to-Prototype Loop

**Research → Record → Design → Prototype → Playtest → Identify unknowns → Research again**

This keeps scholarship connected to the actual game.
