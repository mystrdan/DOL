# DOL Civilizations Foundation

## Status
Phase 0 — Step 2: World Foundation

## Core Rule

DOL contains fictional civilizations inspired by researched African histories, cultures, environments, artistic traditions, technologies, languages, and social systems.

A DOL civilization is never a claim that several real-world peoples were historically one civilization.

**Research first. Fiction second.**

## 1. Civilization Architecture

Each civilization receives a research and design record containing:

- Name
- Region
- Environment
- Historical inspirations
- Cultural inspirations
- Language inspirations
- Architecture
- Clothing
- Food
- Music and instruments
- Art and symbols
- Economy
- Trade
- Political structure
- Social structure
- Military traditions
- Technology
- Spiritual worldview
- Mythological sources
- What is documented
- What is interpreted
- What is invented for DOL
- Sensitivity / representation notes

## 2. Longlat Civilization

Working identity:

**Longlat Civilization**

Region:
- The Heartlands

Environment:
- Major river
- fertile surrounding lands
- nearby hills/highlands
- intersection of old trade routes

Character:
- cosmopolitan
- commercially active
- intellectually curious
- politically complex
- historically connected to surrounding peoples

Longlat should feel like a place where different people genuinely meet.

### Social layers

Working groups:

- farmers
- fishers
- artisans
- merchants
- scholars
- builders
- guards and soldiers
- healers
- religious/spiritual specialists
- political leaders
- travelers and foreigners

These are functional categories, not rigid castes.

## 4. Civilizational Relationships

The continent should not be divided into isolated fantasy kingdoms.

Civilizations interact through:

- trade;
- migration;
- diplomacy;
- marriage;
- scholarship;
- conflict;
- religious exchange;
- technology;
- art;
- language;
- exploration.

Some relationships should be peaceful.

Others should be tense.

None should be reduced to a simplistic "good kingdom vs evil kingdom" structure.

## 5. Language

DOL should not invent one generic "African language."

Each major fictional civilization can have its own constructed language or naming system, informed by appropriate real-world linguistic research.

For early development:

- interface language can remain English;
- character names can follow each civilization's established naming system;
- important fictional terms should have consistent phonology and spelling.

## 6. Visual Identity

Every civilization needs a recognizable visual language.

This should emerge from:

- environment;
- available materials;
- architecture;
- clothing;
- craft;
- technology;
- social function;
- history.

Avoid making visual identity depend only on color palettes or stereotypes.

## 7. Mythology Integration

Mythological material is researched separately.

A civilization may interact with one or more mythological traditions, but DOL must preserve distinctions between traditions.

For every supernatural element, record:

**Source → Context → Documentation → DOL adaptation → Gameplay role**

## 8. Game 1 Civilization Scope

The first playable experience should primarily involve:

1. Longlat Civilization
2. One nearby civilization
3. One more distant civilization introduced through travelers, trade, lore, or later gameplay

Additional civilizations can appear as the world expands.

## 9. Current Canon

Canon:
- DOL contains multiple fictional civilizations.
- Longlat is the first major civilization.
- Neighboring civilizations are geographically and culturally distinct.
- Trade and interaction connect the world.
- Real-world inspirations must be documented separately from fictional invention.

Open:
- final names;
- languages;
- rulers;
- political systems;
- architecture;
- clothing;
- symbols;
- historical conflicts;
- exact real-world research sources;
- final Game 1 civilization selection.
