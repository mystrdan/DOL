# DOL Game Bible

## 1. Working title

**Dawn of Longlat**

Short form: **DOL**

The title refers to the beginning of a new age in the fictional world of Longlat.

## 2. Creative premise

DOL is an original fantasy game set in a fictional African-inspired world whose history intersects with multiple distinct African mythological and cultural traditions.

The game must never imply that all African traditions are one mythology. Real-world traditions remain distinct; the fictional story creates connections between them.

## 3. Story seed

For generations, the boundary between the mortal world and ancient spiritual realms has remained stable. Then something changes. Sacred places behave strangely. Forgotten beings return. Old powers awaken. Stories once thought to belong only to the past begin appearing in the present.

At the center of the disturbance is Longlat.

The player enters a world where the coming dawn may either restore the old order or begin an entirely new age.

**Central question:** What happens when worlds that were never meant to meet finally collide?

This is only the story seed. No major mythology, character, villain, or historical tradition is locked yet.

## 4. The Longlat rule

Longlat is fictional. We may create its geography, communities, politics, characters, creatures, conflicts, organizations, histories, technology, magic, cities and artifacts.

Real African traditions can inspire and inform the world, but fictional elements must be clearly treated as DOL fiction.

## 5. Mythology framework

Every researched tradition will be documented separately using:

TRADITION → COSMOLOGY → FIGURES → SPIRITS → CREATURES → ARTIFACTS → STORIES → SYMBOLS → PLACES → CONCEPTS

Each entry should distinguish documented tradition, interpretation, uncertainty or variation, DOL adaptation, and completely original DOL invention.

## 6. Player experience

The player should feel curiosity, discovery, wonder, danger, growth, choice and connection.

## 7. Design pillars

### Exploration
The world rewards looking, listening, investigating and learning.

### Combat
Combat must be readable, satisfying and mobile-friendly. It should serve the story rather than exist only as spectacle.

### Discovery
Mythology should be encountered and understood, not dumped on the player.

### Identity
Characters need distinct motivations, mechanics, visual language and cultural context.

### Consequence
Important player actions should have understandable consequences.

## 8. Development philosophy

Build vertically, not horizontally. First prove that one small piece of DOL is fun. Then expand.

## 9. Five project roles

**Story Writer:** Does the narrative work?

**Game Designer:** Is it fun and do mechanics serve the player?

**Developer:** Can it realistically be built and maintained?

**Player:** Is it understandable, enjoyable and compelling?

**Tester:** What breaks, confuses, frustrates or exploits the design?

A feature is successful only when it survives all five perspectives.

## 10. Current canon

- Title: Dawn of Longlat
- Abbreviation: DOL
- Longlat is fictional
- The project draws from multiple African traditions
- Research comes before adaptation
- First release is mobile-first
- The game is designed for future expansion

Everything else remains open for discovery.
