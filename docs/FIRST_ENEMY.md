# DOL First Enemy Foundation

## Purpose

Define the first combat threat without prematurely turning a real-world sacred being into a monster.

## Enemy Rule

The first supernatural enemy should be **DOL original**.

This gives the prototype freedom to establish combat language without misrepresenting a documented spiritual being.

## Working Concept

**The Veil-Torn**

A creature created by prolonged exposure to a damaged boundary.

It is not a god, ancestor, or documented spirit.

### Visual Direction

The creature should look like something that has been distorted by the collision between worlds:
- recognizably physical silhouette;
- one or two impossible features;
- movement that changes near the Veil;
- readable attack telegraphs;
- strong silhouette on a mobile screen.

Avoid generic “African monster” stereotypes.

## Combat Role

The first enemy teaches:
- spacing;
- attack timing;
- dodging;
- recovery;
- reading telegraphed attacks.

It should have a small moveset.

### Prototype Moves

1. **Rush** — closes distance.
2. **Swipe** — short-range attack.
3. **Veil Pulse** — telegraphed area attack.
4. **Retreat** — briefly disengages when damaged.

No complicated status effects yet.

## Narrative Function

The enemy proves that the Dawn has physical consequences.

It should also leave the player wondering:

> What else can cross the boundary?

## Future Research Gate

Later DOL enemies may be inspired by documented creatures or supernatural beings, but only after their individual cultural context has been researched and an adaptation record has been completed.

**The first enemy teaches the game. It does not teach the player a mythology lecture.**
