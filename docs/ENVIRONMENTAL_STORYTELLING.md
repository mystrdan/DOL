# DOL Environmental Storytelling Rules

## Purpose

Make the world communicate history and culture through space rather than exposition.

## Rule 1 — Every Place Has a Reason

Buildings, roads, fields, crossings, shrines/sacred spaces, markets and homes should exist because people use them.

Ask:

**Who built this? Who uses it? What problem does it solve? What changed?**

## Rule 2 — Show Use Before Decoration

A cooking area should communicate cooking.

A market should communicate exchange.

A road should communicate movement.

A worship space should communicate community life.

Do not add cultural objects merely because they look visually distinctive.

## Rule 3 — Layer Time

The Marches should contain:

- old construction;
- current use;
- recent damage;
- signs of repair;
- abandoned elements.

This makes Longlat feel older than the player's arrival.

## Rule 4 — Material Language

Choose materials according to:
- environment;
- availability;
- technology;
- trade;
- maintenance;
- household economics.

Do not choose materials from a generic “African architecture” mood board.

## Rule 5 — People Explain Spaces

A structure becomes meaningful when the player sees someone using it.

Examples:
- someone repairing a tool;
- someone preparing food;
- someone carrying water;
- traders arranging goods;
- children playing;
- people gathering;
- travelers waiting.

## Rule 6 — Supernatural Contrast

The Dawn becomes stronger when normal life is established first.

Normal:
- birds;
- traffic;
- conversation;
- work.

Wrong:
- silence;
- impossible movement;
- misplaced objects;
- environmental repetition;
- unexplained absence.

## Rule 7 — Avoid Lore Objects Everywhere

Not every object should be a collectible.

The player should sometimes simply observe.

## Rule 8 — Cultural Attribution

When a real-world object, building form, symbol, practice, textile, tool, food, musical form or language element becomes a meaningful game asset, the reference record should identify:

- community;
- region;
- source;
- context;
- variation;
- fictionalization;
- adaptation status.

## Core Test

Remove the supernatural effects.

Would the settlement still look like a place where people could actually live?

If not, the worldbuilding is too dependent on fantasy decoration.
