# DOL Vertical Slice — Longlat Marches

## Purpose

This document defines the smallest playable section that can prove Dawn of Longlat works as a game.

It is deliberately small.

## Player Journey

**Arrival → Discovery → Disturbance → Veil → Encounter → Escape/Win → Reveal**

### Beat 1 — Arrival

The player enters the outskirts of Longlat.

No exposition dump.

The environment establishes:
- people living normally;
- a road or settlement;
- evidence of a wider world;
- one subtle sign that something is wrong.

### Beat 2 — Discovery

The player notices an abnormal environmental event.

Examples:
- animals reacting to something unseen;
- an impossible sound;
- a damaged boundary marker;
- a person reporting a strange occurrence.

Keep the event short and visual.

### Beat 3 — Disturbance

The player follows a simple lead toward the damaged Veil.

This is where exploration and navigation are tested.

### Beat 4 — Veil

The player reaches the first supernatural boundary.

The Veil should be visually memorable but mechanically simple.

It introduces one DOL-original rule:

> The world behaves differently near a damaged boundary.

Examples for prototype testing:
- sound changes;
- paths appear/disappear;
- objects react;
- enemy presence becomes possible.

Only one or two effects should be implemented initially.

### Beat 5 — Encounter

A first enemy emerges.

The enemy should be an original DOL creature unless a researched tradition has already passed the adaptation gate.

Combat teaches:
- attack;
- defense/dodge;
- positioning;
- recovery.

The enemy should be simple enough that failure teaches the player something.

### Beat 6 — Resolution

The player wins or escapes.

No loot explosion.

Reward:
- progression;
- information;
- access to the next route.

### Beat 7 — Reveal

The player sees evidence that the Veil disturbance is not isolated.

End the slice on a question, not a lore lecture:

> If this boundary is breaking here, where else is it happening?

## Prototype Map

A compact map can use:

- Spawn
- Settlement edge
- Road
- Exploration pocket
- River/bridge
- Veil site
- Combat arena
- Safe return point

Target: one short session.

## Prototype Metrics

Measure:
- time to first movement;
- time to first discovery;
- time to first combat;
- number of deaths;
- where players get lost;
- whether players understand the objective;
- whether combat feels responsive;
- whether players voluntarily explore.

Do not optimize for length yet.

## Hard Scope Limit

The first slice should not require:
- a full city;
- a full mythology system;
- multiple playable characters;
- multiple regions;
- elaborate crafting;
- online systems.

## Definition of Done

The slice is done when a player can start, understand, explore, encounter, fight, finish, and restart it without developer assistance.
