# DOL Atlas — World Map Foundation

## Status
Phase 0 — Step 2: World Foundation

## Purpose

The DOL Atlas defines the physical geography of the fictional world before detailed character, faction, or combat design.

The map is fictional. Real African geography and cultures may inspire environmental and cultural design, but DOL must not present its fictional geography as historical Africa.

## 1. The Great Continent

Working name: **The Great Continent**

Final name: OPEN

The continent is large enough to support multiple distinct climates, civilizations, belief systems, trade networks, languages, and supernatural traditions.

### Major regions — working structure

1. **The Heartlands**
   - Longlat and its surrounding territories.
   - Major crossroads of people and ideas.
   - Primary setting for the beginning of the story.

2. **The Green Expanse**
   - Vast forest region.
   - Dense vegetation, rivers, hidden settlements, ancient paths.
   - Strong supernatural presence.

3. **The Golden Savannah**
   - Open grasslands and seasonal rivers.
   - Large kingdoms, horse/cattle cultures, hunters, warriors, and traders.
   - Important overland routes.

4. **The Red Highlands**
   - Mountainous territory.
   - High plateaus, cliffs, caves, old sanctuaries, and isolated communities.
   - Ancient ruins may contain evidence about the boundaries between worlds.

5. **The Great Sands**
   - Northern desert region.
   - Oasis cities and long-distance caravan routes.
   - Buried ruins and forgotten roads.

6. **The Riverlands**
   - Major river systems and fertile floodplains.
   - Agricultural civilizations and major settlements.
   - Rivers serve as both transportation routes and culturally significant locations.

7. **The Southern Wilds**
   - Diverse grasslands, woodland, wetlands, and rocky terrain.
   - Remote communities and old hunting grounds.
   - One of the regions most affected by creatures emerging after the Dawn.

8. **The Outer Coasts**
   - Eastern and western coastal zones.
   - Ports, islands, fishing communities, merchants, and maritime routes.
   - Connect distant parts of the continent.

## 2. Longlat's Position

Longlat sits within **The Heartlands**, positioned near the intersection of major land and river routes.

Its strategic importance comes from geography rather than imperial ownership of the entire continent.

Longlat connects:

- inland settlements;
- river routes;
- savannah trade;
- forest routes;
- mountain passes;
- eventually, coastal trade.

## 3. Geographic Design Rule

The regions should not form a perfect radial map around Longlat.

The continent must feel naturally shaped.

Rivers should follow terrain.

Mountain ranges should influence climate and travel.

Deserts should have ecological and geographic reasons.

Settlements should develop around water, agriculture, trade, defensive terrain, resources, or sacred locations.

## 4. Supernatural Geography

The supernatural does not replace physical geography.

Instead, it overlays it.

Some physical locations may have unusually thin boundaries between worlds.

Working terms:

- **Veils** — places where the boundary is unusually thin.
- **Crossings** — stable routes between layers of reality.
- **Wounds** — places damaged by the Dawn.
- **Anchors** — locations that stabilize a region's boundary.
- **Dead Zones** — places where supernatural influence has collapsed or become strangely absent.

These are DOL concepts and are not claims about any real-world religious tradition.

## 5. Longlat — Preliminary Geographic Identity

Longlat should be built around three features:

### The River

A major river passes near or through the civilization.

It supports agriculture, transportation, trade, and settlement.

### The Heights

Elevated terrain or nearby hills provide natural defense and a visual landmark.

### The Old Road

An ancient route passes through Longlat and predates the current civilization.

The Old Road may eventually prove to be more than a trade route.

## 6. The First Playable Region

Working name: **The Longlat Marches**

This is the territory immediately surrounding Longlat.

It should contain enough environmental variety for the first game slice without requiring an enormous open world.

Possible locations:

- Longlat city;
- farming settlements;
- river crossing;
- forest edge;
- abandoned shrine/ruin;
- old road;
- small wilderness zone;
- first Veil;
- first Wound.

## 7. Map Scale

DOL should avoid designing an enormous world before gameplay exists.

For the first playable release:

**One region should feel large enough to explore but small enough to finish.**

The complete continent can expand later through additional regions, games, or major updates.

## 8. Cartography Rule

Every location added to the map should answer at least one question:

- Why is it here?
- Who lives here?
- What resources does it provide?
- What route does it control?
- What happened here?
- What cultural meaning does it have?
- What gameplay does it enable?
- What story does it reveal?

## 9. Current Map Status

Canon:
- Longlat is in the Heartlands.
- Longlat is a crossroads.
- A major river is associated with Longlat.
- An ancient road passes through the region.
- The supernatural overlays the physical world.
- The Longlat Marches are the working first playable region.

Not yet canon:
- final continent name;
- final regional names;
- exact river;
- exact mountain ranges;
- exact borders;
- exact city layout;
- final cosmological terminology.
