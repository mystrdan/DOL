# DOL World Bible

## Status
Phase 0 — Step 2: World Foundation

## 1. World Premise

**Dawn of Longlat** takes place in an original fictional world whose civilizations, places, and supernatural systems are invented for DOL while drawing carefully researched inspiration from many African mythological traditions.

Longlat is **not** a historical name for Africa, and the fictional world must never imply that Africa's diverse traditions were one unified mythology.

**Core rule:** Real traditions remain distinct. DOL creates the fictional bridge.

## 2. The World Structure

The setting is built in three layers:

### The Mortal World
The physical world of peoples, kingdoms, cities, wilderness, trade routes, wars, families, and ordinary life.

### The Hidden Realms
Spiritual and supernatural domains that exist alongside the mortal world. Different cultures may understand these domains differently; DOL may represent that diversity through multiple realms rather than forcing everything into one hierarchy.

### The Between
A dangerous boundary-space where the mortal and hidden worlds can overlap. This becomes important after the Dawn.

## 3. Longlat

Working definition:

> **Longlat is an ancient civilization and crossroads at the heart of the DOL world, built where several great regions and supernatural pathways meet.**

Longlat is not the entire continent. It is one place within a much larger world.

Its importance comes from its position: historically, Longlat became a meeting point for peoples, merchants, scholars, warriors, mystics, and travelers. Beneath the civilization lies an older mystery connected to the boundaries between worlds.

## 4. The Continent

Working name: **The Great Continent**

The final continental name is intentionally undecided.

The continent should contain radically different environments and civilizations:

- coastal regions
- river civilizations
- savannahs
- dense forests
- highlands
- deserts
- wetlands
- islands
- ancient ruins
- major trading routes

These regions should feel geographically connected without becoming culturally interchangeable.

## 5. Peoples and Civilizations

DOL will use fictional peoples and civilizations.

When a fictional culture is inspired by a real tradition, the design document must record:

1. The real tradition or cultural context.
2. What is historically/documentarily supported.
3. What visual, linguistic, philosophical, or environmental inspiration is being used.
4. What DOL changes.
5. Why the change exists.
6. What is completely fictional.

This prevents accidental presentation of fictional material as authentic mythology.

## 6. Cosmology Rule

There is no single DOL statement that says all African gods, spirits, ancestors, and creatures belong to one historical pantheon.

Instead:

**Many traditions → many understandings → one fictional world → one original story.**

The game can allow supernatural encounters to coexist because of DOL's fictional world event, not because the source traditions were historically one system.

## 7. The Dawn

The central catastrophe is currently called **The Dawn**.

The Dawn begins when the boundary separating the Mortal World from the Hidden Realms is damaged.

Consequences:

- sacred places begin behaving abnormally;
- supernatural beings can cross boundaries more easily;
- forgotten paths become accessible;
- ancient powers awaken;
- different regions experience different manifestations;
- ordinary people are forced to confront forces previously separated from daily life.

The Dawn is not simply an apocalypse. It is a **collision of worlds**.

## 8. The Central Mystery

The player initially believes the Dawn may be a natural cosmic event.

Later, evidence suggests that someone or something caused it.

The deeper mystery:

> **Why were the boundaries created in the first place — and who benefits from breaking them?**

## 9. The Antagonistic Force

The final antagonist is not yet canonically named.

Working concept:

An ancient primordial intelligence believes the separation between worlds was a mistake. It seeks to collapse the boundaries permanently, believing this will restore a more complete and powerful world.

Its philosophy should be understandable even when its actions are destructive.

The antagonist should not be a real-world deity presented as evil.

## 10. The Player Character

The protagonist will initially be an original mortal character.

They should have a personal reason to investigate the Dawn before becoming a legendary figure.

Their identity, culture, abilities, home region, and backstory remain open until the world and research foundations are stronger.

## 11. Game 1 Scope

DOL's universe can eventually contain many African-inspired regions and traditions.

The first game should **not** attempt to represent the entire continent.

Game 1 should establish:

- Longlat;
- the first surrounding region;
- a small number of deeply researched traditions;
- the first supernatural realm;
- the Dawn event;
- the protagonist;
- the first major enemy faction;
- the first playable vertical slice.

Expansion can then introduce additional regions, traditions, characters, creatures, and realms.

## 12. World-Building Checklist

Before moving to detailed character design, we need to lock:

- [ ] Longlat's physical location
- [ ] Longlat's visual identity
- [ ] surrounding regions
- [ ] major geographic features
- [ ] peoples/civilizations
- [ ] historical timeline
- [ ] cosmology
- [ ] rules of supernatural interaction
- [ ] cause of the Dawn
- [ ] first playable location
- [ ] protagonist's starting point

## Development Principle

**Research first. Fiction second. Gameplay third.**

Gameplay can simplify systems for fun, but it should not erase the cultural distinctions that inspired them.
