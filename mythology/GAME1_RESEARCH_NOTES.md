# Game 1 Research Notes — Current Pass

## Purpose

This file records the evidence-backed research direction that should run alongside prototype development.

DOL does **not** treat “African mythology” as one mythology. Each adaptation must remain attached to a specific community/tradition and its evidence.

## Current research anchors

### Akan-speaking peoples

Akan research is especially useful for understanding how spiritual, social, ancestral, land, and human concepts can overlap without turning the world into a generic spell system.

Current evidence to investigate further:

- Cambridge's discussion of Akan theories of personhood describes physical and spiritual elements associated with conception, including **bogya/mogya, sunsum, and kra**, while emphasizing their relationship to social, natural, and spiritual worlds.
- Metropolitan Museum material on Akan memorial heads documents sacred contexts connected to royal ancestors and remembrance.
- Metropolitan Museum material on Asante-Akan akua 'ba figures provides evidence for specific symbolic and devotional contexts, including associations discussed around Asase Ya.

### Yoruba peoples

Yoruba research is useful as a comparative case for sacred landscapes, shrines, divination, kingship, and relationships between visible and spiritual life. It must not be merged with Akan material.

Current evidence to investigate further:

- The Metropolitan Museum describes plurality within Yoruba views while documenting the religious and political importance of Ile-Ife.
- Museum collections document shrines, sacred places, devotional objects, and specific orisa traditions.
- The evidence reinforces the rule that a shrine, deity, ritual object, or sacred landscape should retain its specific context rather than become a generic fantasy prop.

Real-world traditions should initially influence **research-informed social/environmental context**, not be copied into the first enemy.

## Research gates before final art

Before a sacred place, religious symbol, deity, spirit, ritual object, language phrase, or ceremonial practice enters final game art or mechanics:

1. Identify the specific community/tradition.
2. Record the source and exact evidence.
3. Check for variation or contested interpretation.
4. Classify it as DOCUMENTED, INTERPRETATION, DOL ORIGINAL, or UNVERIFIED.
5. Record the adaptation decision and representation risk.
6. Review the result before final art.
