# Amazigh Traditions — Preliminary Research Record

> **Status: Research draft — not DOL canon.**

## 1. Identity & Scope

“Amazigh” refers to diverse Indigenous peoples of North Africa, with substantial linguistic, regional, and historical diversity across present-day Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, and parts of the Sahara/Sahel.

This record must not treat Amazigh peoples as one homogeneous culture.

Research should distinguish:
- regional communities
- Tamazight language varieties
- ancient, medieval, and contemporary contexts
- Indigenous traditions and later Islamic influences
- archaeological evidence from later folkloric interpretation

## 2. Research Areas

Investigate:
- pre-Islamic religious traditions
- ancestor and saint traditions where historically relevant
- sacred landscapes, springs, mountains, caves, trees, and stones
- celestial and seasonal symbolism
- protective spirits and supernatural beings
- oral narratives and folklore
- women’s roles in oral and ritual traditions
- rock art and archaeological evidence, without automatically equating imagery with specific later beliefs
- Carthaginian, Roman, Egyptian, and other neighboring influences

## 3. Important Figures/Subjects

Research candidates should include:
- ancient Libyan/Amazigh religious concepts and inscriptions
- local female supernatural figures and folklore
- Amun-related North African evidence where relevant
- Berber/Amazigh saint and shrine traditions in appropriate historical contexts
- regional oral traditions rather than a single “Amazigh pantheon”

No figure is approved for DOL gameplay.

## 4. Representation Rules

DOL must not:
- use “Berber” and “Amazigh” as interchangeable historical labels without context
- invent a universal Amazigh pantheon
- turn contemporary religious communities into fantasy factions
- treat North African culture as merely an Egyptian extension
- use archaeological symbols as fantasy magic without evidence
- erase Islamic-era and modern Amazigh history from the cultural record

## 5. Evidence Strategy

Prioritize archaeology, inscriptions, linguistics, university scholarship, museums, archives, and contemporary Amazigh cultural scholarship.

Ancient religious evidence is often fragmentary. Where evidence is uncertain, mark it **UNVERIFIED** rather than filling gaps with invented mythology.
