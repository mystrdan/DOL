# Ancient Egyptian Religious Traditions — Preliminary Research Record

> **Status: Research draft — not DOL canon.**
>
> Ancient Egyptian religion changed across more than three millennia and across different regions and periods. This record must therefore avoid treating “Egyptian mythology” as one frozen system.

## 1. Research Scope

Study religious traditions from early dynastic Egypt through later periods, with attention to regional temples, changing political contexts, foreign rule, and differences between periods.

Research areas:
- creation traditions and cosmogonies
- divine beings and local cults
- kingship and divine authority
- Ma'at, order, justice, and balance
- death, burial, and afterlife traditions
- temples, ritual, priesthood, and sacred landscapes
- animal symbolism and divine iconography
- solar, celestial, river, desert, and fertility concepts
- Egyptian relationships with Nubia/Kush, Libya, the Levant, and the Mediterranean

These are research subjects, not automatically DOL characters.

## 3. Important Representation Rule

Egyptian religion should not become the generic “ancient African magic” layer of DOL. Its own historical language, institutions, art, geography, and chronology must remain visible.

Do not merge Egyptian traditions with Yoruba, Akan, Nubian/Kushite, or Amazigh traditions merely because they share broad themes.

## 4. Evidence

Prioritize:
1. archaeological evidence and inscriptions
2. papyri and ancient texts
3. museum and university collections
4. Egyptological scholarship
5. comparative research used carefully

A strong continent-wide research reference is Robert M. Baum, *Ancient African Religions: A History* (2024), whose scope includes ancient Egypt within a broader African religious history. citeturn0search4
