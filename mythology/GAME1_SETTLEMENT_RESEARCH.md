# Game 1 Settlement Research Notes

## Purpose
Build the ordinary world around Longlat before adding culturally specific final assets.

## Research findings

University of Ghana research on Katamansu combines oral history, ethnographic and historical evidence, and archaeology to reconstruct settlement history and examines material evidence associated with a Ga shrine context.

Source: https://pure.ug.edu.gh/en/publications/material-culture-and-indigenous-spiritism-the-katamansu-archaeolo/

Recent archaeological work at Peki uses sacred sites, burial contexts, domestic middens, oral traditions, and historical sources to examine political authority, consumption, and everyday life. Material remains are interpreted within specific historical contexts rather than as a single generic category.

Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10761-026-00834-9

University of Ghana research on the Ghanaian compound house describes it as a prominent urban and traditional housing type and emphasizes its role beyond shelter: security, memory, relationships, and everyday social life can become attached to the shared space. Ethnoarchaeological research on Old Buipe documents how buildings, compounds, materials, environmental conditions, social organization, and economic life interact. It also shows that building forms and materials change over time, including movement from mud/daub and thatch toward cement and metal roofing. citeturn1search2turn1search6

Research on Ghanaian housing and resettlement further shows that shared spaces and resident participation can contribute to a sense of ownership, community, and identity. This supports treating Longlat's common yard as a lived social space rather than a decorative background. citeturn1search3turn1search5

The Metropolitan Museum's documentation of surviving Asante traditional buildings highlights central courtyards, adjoining sections, community continuity, and the coexistence of architectural and spiritual functions. DOL should treat this as a specific Asante reference, not as a generic model for Longlat. citeturn1search4

## DOL implications

- Settlement design must start from a named fictional community and a documented regional reference set.
- Everyday objects need a reason to exist: work, food, storage, transport, trade, water, gathering, worship, or household life.
- Shared yards should have social purpose: conversation, work, movement, waiting, children, visitors, or household activity.
- Architecture should show change over time rather than a frozen “traditional Africa” aesthetic.
- Archaeological or sacred objects should not be added only because they look visually interesting.
- Greybox spaces should communicate function before specific cultural assets are selected.
- Final asset references must record provenance and adaptation risk.

## Current prototype decision

Use functional greybox shapes now. Delay culturally specific final props, architecture, clothing, symbols, ritual objects, and language until the relevant community/tradition and evidence are fixed.
