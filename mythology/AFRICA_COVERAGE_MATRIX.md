# AFRICA COVERAGE MATRIX

> **Purpose:** prevent DOL from treating a short list of famous mythologies as if it represents Africa. This is a living research index, not a claim that every community shares one religion or mythology.

## Core Rule
Africa does not have one indigenous religion or one mythology. Scholarship emphasizes many locally embedded traditions with substantial variation between peoples and regions. DOL therefore uses a coverage-first system:

**Community / ethnolinguistic group → specific tradition(s) → sources → variations → classification → adaptation decision**

A broad label such as Bantu, Nilotic, Berber/Amazigh, Mande, San, or West African is never treated as a single mythology.

## Coverage Layers

### North Africa & Sahara
Egyptian; Amazigh communities; Tuareg/Tamasheq communities; Nubian communities; Kushite/Nile Valley traditions; Saharan and Sahelian communities requiring separate research; and additional local traditions identified through ethnographic, archaeological, linguistic, and community sources.

### West Africa
Akan communities; Yoruba; Igbo; Edo/Benin; Fon; Ewe; Mande-related communities; Hausa; Fulani/Fula; Wolof; Serer; Songhai; Kanuri; Dogon; and additional Atlantic, Gur, Kwa, Mande, Chadic, Nilo-Saharan, and other communities requiring separate research.

### Central Africa
Kongo; Luba; Lunda; Chokwe; Fang; Beti; Mongo; Mangbetu; selected forest peoples including Mbuti communities; and additional Central African communities requiring dedicated records rather than a single Bantu mythology.

### East Africa & Horn
Oromo; Somali; Maasai; Dinka; Nuer; Baganda; Gikuyu/Kikuyu; Luo communities; Kalenjin communities; Sukuma; Chaga; Amhara; Tigrinya/Eritrean communities; and additional Cushitic, Nilotic, Omotic, Bantu, and Horn communities requiring separate research.

### Southern Africa
Zulu; Xhosa; Shona; Lozi; Bemba; Makonde; San communities including !Kung/Ju|'hoansi and others; Sotho; Tswana; Venda; Ndebele; and additional southern African communities requiring community-specific research.

### Islands & Indian Ocean
Madagascar/Malagasy traditions; Comorian traditions; Swahili Coast and island traditions; Seychelles/Mauritius historical-cultural traditions where relevant; and additional Indian Ocean communities requiring dedicated research.

## Exhaustiveness Protocol
“Do not omit any tribe” is interpreted operationally as **do not silently omit communities once they are identified as relevant to the research scope**. A finite document cannot responsibly claim that a few dozen entries equal every African people: the continent contains very large ethnolinguistic diversity, and classifications change by source and by whether subgroup, language, clan, nationality, or ethnic identity is being counted.

For every region, future research passes must:
1. Start from authoritative ethnolinguistic and ethnographic reference works.
2. Build a directory of named peoples and subgroups.
3. Mark each entry NOT_STARTED, DISCOVERY, SOURCE_REVIEW, RESEARCH_READY, ADAPTATION_REVIEW, or DEFERRED.
4. Split an umbrella entry whenever evidence shows materially different traditions.
5. Record alternate names and spelling variants so communities are not duplicated or lost through naming differences.
6. Include living/community perspectives wherever possible.
7. Record traditions that have changed through Christianity, Islam, colonialism, migration, diaspora, or modern state formation rather than freezing them in a precolonial fantasy.

## What Counts as Mythology
DOL will not limit the database to gods. Relevant material may include creation/origin narratives; cosmology and sacred geography; divine beings and other spiritual agents; ancestors and the dead; heroes, founders, tricksters and legendary figures; spirits; oral epics, folktales, praise traditions, songs and performance; sacred places, objects, symbols, animals, plants and material culture; ritual specialists, divination, healing, initiation, festivals and rites where ethically and academically appropriate; philosophical and moral concepts; and historical memory where it intersects with mythic or sacred narratives.

## Evidence Rule
Oxford scholarship stresses that African indigenous religions are numerous and locally embedded, and that evidence may be archaeological, historical, ethnographic, archival, oral, or community-based. ## Adaptation Rule
Research breadth does **not** mean putting every deity, spirit, ritual, symbol, or sacred object into combat. Some material should remain research/lore only. Living traditions receive special care, and no community should be represented as a monolith.

## Current DOL Principle
**Research first. Fiction second. Gameplay third.**

This matrix is intentionally expandable. The research repository should grow toward comprehensive continental coverage instead of pretending that a fixed shortlist is complete.
