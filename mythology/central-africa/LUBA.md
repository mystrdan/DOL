# Luba traditions

> **Research status: Preliminary — not DOL canon.**

This research track exists to prevent DOL from collapsing distinct African peoples, languages, histories, and spiritual traditions into a single mythology. DOL uses this material only after source verification and contextual review.

## Scope
- Identify the specific communities, language varieties, historical regions, and internal subgroups relevant to this track.
- Record regional and historical variation rather than assuming one uniform belief system.
- Separate indigenous religious traditions from later Christian, Islamic, colonial, national, and diaspora influences while documenting their interactions.

## Research Areas
- Cosmology and creation/origin traditions
- Supreme-being concepts and other spiritual beings, where documented
- Ancestors, spirits, heroes, tricksters, and other supernatural figures
- Sacred places, landscapes, animals, plants, objects, symbols, and material culture
- Divination, healing, initiation, festivals, rites, specialists, and institutions where appropriate
- Oral literature, praise traditions, epics, folktales, historical traditions, and performance
- Historical change, migration, trade, political systems, and religious interaction
- Contemporary communities and living interpretations

## Evidence Standard
Prioritize:
1. Community and cultural institutions
2. Academic monographs and peer-reviewed research
3. Museums, archives, libraries, and archaeological publications
4. Primary or early historical/ethnographic sources, read critically
5. Carefully evaluated secondary scholarship

Do not treat unsourced mythology lists, social posts, AI summaries, or recycled web lists as sufficient evidence.

## DOL Classification
Every future claim or adaptation should be marked:
- **DOCUMENTED** — supported by reliable evidence
- **INTERPRETATION** — scholarly or comparative interpretation
- **DOL ORIGINAL** — invented for Dawn of Longlat
- **UNVERIFIED** — requires further research

## Adaptation Gate
No documented sacred figure, ritual, symbol, object, or story becomes a DOL boss, weapon, spell, costume, faction, or collectible merely because it is visually interesting. Any adaptation must record its source, context, changes, reason for adaptation, intended gameplay/story role, and representation concerns.

## Open Research Questions
- Which specific communities should be represented rather than using a broad umbrella label?
- Which sources are community-authored or community-reviewed?
- Which elements have strong documentation and which remain disputed?
- What regional variations materially change interpretation?
- Which material should remain cultural/lore reference rather than gameplay?
- What fictional DOL elements can be inspired by the research without claiming historical authenticity?

## DOL Rule
**Research first. Fiction second. Gameplay third.**
