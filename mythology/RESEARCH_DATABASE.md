# DOL Mythology Research Database

> **Purpose:** A structured index for researching African mythological and religious traditions before any adaptation into *Dawn of Longlat*.
>
> **Rule:** Research first. Fiction second. Gameplay third.

## Why this database exists

DOL draws inspiration from many African traditions. This database prevents those traditions from being flattened into one fictional “African mythology.”

Each tradition remains connected to its own:

- people/community
- region
- language
- historical context
- worldview
- stories
- spiritual beings
- symbols
- material culture
- sources

DOL may create fictional connections between researched traditions inside its own universe, but those fictional connections must never be presented as historical fact.

---

## Research Record Standard

Every tradition should eventually have a record containing:

### 1. Identity
- Tradition/community
- People(s)
- Region
- Languages
- Historical context
- Contemporary/living context

### 2. Cosmology & Worldview
- Creation/origin concepts
- Structure of the cosmos
- Supreme/creator concepts
- Deities/divinities
- Spirits
- Ancestors
- Heroes
- Supernatural beings
- Afterlife concepts
- Moral/philosophical concepts

### 3. Mythic Material
- Major stories
- Trickster figures
- Creatures
- Sacred objects
- Weapons
- Places
- Symbols
- Ritual/context where appropriate

### 4. Evidence
For every significant claim, record:
- Source
- Author/institution
- Publication/date
- Page or section where possible
- Source type
- Confidence
- Contradictory or variant accounts

### 5. DOL Classification

Every researched element must receive one status:

**DOCUMENTED**  
Supported by the research record.

**INTERPRETATION**  
A scholarly or analytical interpretation that should not automatically be treated as literal traditional belief.

**DOL ORIGINAL**  
Created by the game team.

**UNVERIFIED**  
Interesting claim that has not yet met DOL's source standard.

### 6. Adaptation Record

If something enters the game:

**Source element → Context → What DOL changes → Why → Story role → Gameplay role → Visual role → Representation concerns**

No adaptation should silently erase its source context.

# DOL Research Rules

## Rule 1 — No pan-African mashup

Do not combine unrelated traditions simply because they are all African.

## Rule 2 — Attribution matters

If an element comes from a particular people, community, region, or historical tradition, record that explicitly.

## Rule 3 — Living traditions matter

African religious and cultural traditions are not merely ancient fantasy sourcebooks. Many continue to exist in living communities.

## Rule 4 — Colonial sources require critical reading

Older ethnographic sources can contain valuable documentation while also reflecting the assumptions of their authors and period.

## Rule 5 — Don't turn every spiritual being into a combat character

A documented spiritual figure does not automatically become a boss, fighter, enemy, summon, or collectible.

## Rule 6 — Sacred objects are not generic loot

An object with religious, political, ancestral, or ceremonial significance must be researched before being converted into an item or gameplay mechanic.

## Rule 7 — Fiction can connect what history did not

Longlat can create fictional relationships between traditions, but the game must make it clear through its worldbuilding that these are relationships inside DOL.
