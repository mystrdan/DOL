# Makonde traditions

> **Research status: Preliminary — not DOL canon.**

This track exists to keep distinct African peoples, languages, histories, and spiritual traditions from being collapsed into one mythology.

## Research Areas
- Community, language, region, history, and internal variation
- Cosmology, origin traditions, supreme-being concepts, spirits, ancestors, heroes, and tricksters where documented
- Sacred places, landscapes, animals, plants, objects, symbols, and material culture
- Divination, healing, initiation, festivals, rites, specialists, and institutions where appropriate
- Oral literature, epics, folktales, praise traditions, performance, and historical traditions
- Migration, trade, political history, Christianity/Islam, colonial encounters, and contemporary change

## Evidence Standard
Prioritize community/cultural institutions; academic and peer-reviewed scholarship; museums, archives, libraries, and archaeology; and primary or early sources read critically. Unsourced mythology lists, social posts, AI summaries, and recycled web lists are not sufficient evidence.

## Classification
**DOCUMENTED** = supported evidence. **INTERPRETATION** = scholarly/comparative interpretation. **DOL ORIGINAL** = invented for DOL. **UNVERIFIED** = requires research.

## Adaptation Gate
No sacred figure, ritual, symbol, object, or story becomes a DOL boss, weapon, spell, costume, faction, or collectible merely because it is visually interesting. Record source, context, changes, reason, gameplay/story role, and representation concerns before adaptation.

## Open Questions
- Which specific communities and subgroups should be represented?
- Which sources are community-authored or community-reviewed?
- What regional variations materially change interpretation?
- What should remain lore/reference rather than gameplay?
- What fictional DOL elements can be inspired without claiming historical authenticity?

**DOL rule: Research first. Fiction second. Gameplay third.**
