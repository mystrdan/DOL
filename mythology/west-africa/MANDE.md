# Mande-Related Traditions — Preliminary Research Record

> **Status: Research draft — not DOL canon.**
>
> “Mande” refers to a broad set of historically and culturally related peoples and languages across West Africa. This record is an index for further research, not a claim that Mande communities share one identical mythology or religion.

## 1. Identity & Scope

Mande-related peoples and traditions span parts of present-day Mali, Guinea, Senegal, The Gambia, Côte d’Ivoire, Burkina Faso, Sierra Leone, Liberia, and neighboring areas.

Important research communities include, among others:

- Mandinka
- Malinke/Maninka
- Bambara/Bamana
- Dyula/Jula
- Soninke
- Susu/Soussou
- and other Mande-speaking peoples

Historical empires, migrations, trade networks, Islamization, local religious traditions, and colonial-era classification all affect how these traditions are described.

**DOL rule:** Mande is a research family, not a single fictional faction or pantheon.

---

## 2. Worldview & Religious Concepts — Research Targets

Research should distinguish local traditions and historical periods while investigating:

- creator and supreme-being concepts
- spirits and supernatural beings
- ancestors and relationships with the living
- sacred places, landscapes, trees, rivers, and animals
- divination and religious specialists
- initiation and social institutions
- oral history and praise traditions
- relationships between indigenous religious systems and Islam
- ideas concerning destiny, morality, social responsibility, power, and community
- the role of hunters, blacksmiths, musicians, storytellers, and other specialized social roles

Terms and categories should be retained in their local context rather than automatically translated into Western fantasy equivalents.

---

## 3. Oral Tradition & Epic Material

A major research direction is the **Sunjata/Sundiata epic** and its many oral and literary versions.

Research should investigate:

- Sundiata Keita and the formation of the Mali Empire
- the role of oral historians/griots (jeli/jeliw and related traditions)
- political memory and genealogy
- the relationship between historical material and supernatural narrative
- variation between performances and written transcriptions
- figures such as Sumanguru/Soumaoro Kanté and other characters in epic traditions
- how later retellings have changed emphasis

The epic should not be treated as a single fixed canonical text.

**Important:** Historical figures should not automatically become DOL gods, monsters, or fantasy bosses.

---

## 4. Hunters, Blacksmiths, and Specialized Knowledge

Research should examine the cultural roles of:

- **Dozo/donso hunter traditions** and their regional variations
- blacksmith/smith traditions and associated knowledge
- hereditary occupational groups
- musicians, praise singers, oral historians, and specialists in transmitting knowledge

These subjects may provide valuable inspiration for DOL professions, factions, equipment, social systems, and characters.

However, real-world initiation systems, ritual knowledge, and restricted practices should not be copied as generic game mechanics.

---

## 5. Mythic & Supernatural Beings — Research Targets

Potential subjects for individual research include:

- **Maa Ngala** and related creator concepts in Mande scholarship
- **Faro** and traditions concerning water, order, knowledge, or creation, while carefully distinguishing regional interpretations
- **Nyama** and related concepts of vital/spiritual force, noting that translations and scholarly interpretations vary
- supernatural beings associated with forests, rivers, mountains, and other landscapes
- ancestors and protective spiritual forces

These are research subjects only. No item is approved as a DOL character, enemy, boss, spell, or collectible.

---

## 6. Sources & Evidence Strategy

Prioritize:

1. Mande-community and contemporary cultural sources
2. Academic research and university publications
3. Museums, archives, libraries, and historical collections
4. Oral-history and ethnographic documentation
5. Early written accounts, interpreted critically
6. Evaluated secondary sources

### Starting research directions

- D. T. Niane, *Sundiata: An Epic of Old Mali* — useful entry point, while remembering that it is a literary rendering of an oral tradition rather than a single original “mythology book.”
- Marcel Griaule and related Dogon scholarship should **not** automatically be treated as representative of all Mande peoples; community, linguistic, and geographic boundaries must be maintained.
- Further research should prioritize specialist scholarship on specific Mande communities rather than broad “African mythology” summaries.

---

## 7. DOL Classification

Each researched element eventually receives:

- **DOCUMENTED** — supported by reliable evidence.
- **INTERPRETATION** — scholarly interpretation or contested reading.
- **DOL ORIGINAL** — invented specifically for Dawn of Longlat.
- **UNVERIFIED** — encountered but not sufficiently supported.

## 8. Representation Rules

DOL must not:

- collapse all Mande peoples into one culture
- treat the Mali Empire as a fantasy kingdom without historical grounding
- turn every famous historical person into a supernatural character
- treat griots/jeliw as simply “African bards”
- reduce hunter or blacksmith traditions to combat classes
- use Islam and indigenous traditions as a simplistic good-versus-evil conflict
- copy restricted ritual knowledge into gameplay without context
- present one modern retelling as the universal Mande version

---

## 9. Adaptation Gate

Before material enters DOL:

**Specific community → Source → Context → Historical period → Variation → Classification → Adaptation proposal → Review → Gameplay**

For every adaptation, record:

- community/tradition
- geographic context
- historical period
- source
- what is documented
- what is interpretation
- what DOL changes
- why the change exists
- gameplay/story purpose
- representation concerns
- whether an original DOL element would be better

---

## 10. Open Research Questions

1. Which Mande traditions should receive individual records next?
2. How do Mandinka, Maninka, Bambara/Bamana, Soninke, and other traditions differ in cosmology and oral material?
3. Which versions of the Sunjata epic are best documented, and how do they vary?
4. How should oral tradition be represented in a game without pretending there is one definitive text?
5. Which material belongs in environmental/worldbuilding research rather than supernatural gameplay?
6. Which concepts require specialist/community consultation before adaptation?
7. How did Islamization interact with local religious traditions in different Mande societies?
8. Which visual and material traditions can inspire DOL architecture, clothing, weapons, music, and social roles without becoming stereotypes?

---

## 11. DOL Rule

> **Mande-related traditions are a research field, not a single mythology.**

DOL can draw inspiration from documented histories, oral traditions, philosophies, art, environments, technologies, and supernatural concepts while keeping real communities distinct and clearly separating historical material from DOL-original fiction.

**Research first. Fiction second. Gameplay third.**
