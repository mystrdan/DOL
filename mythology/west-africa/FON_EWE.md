# Fon & Ewe Traditions — Preliminary Research Record

> **Status: Research draft — not DOL canon.**
>
> This document is a starting index for research into distinct Fon and Ewe traditions. It must not be treated as a single uniform mythology or as a claim that all Fon- and Ewe-speaking communities share identical beliefs.

## 1. Identity & Scope

### Fon
The Fon are a major cultural and linguistic group historically associated with the Kingdom of Dahomey in the area of present-day Benin. Religious traditions associated with Dahomey include systems commonly discussed under terms such as Vodun/Vodoun, but terminology, practice, and interpretation vary by community and historical period.

### Ewe
Ewe-speaking peoples are distributed across parts of present-day Ghana, Togo, and Benin. Ewe religious traditions contain their own local histories, divine/spiritual categories, ritual systems, oral traditions, and regional variations. They should not be reduced to an extension of Dahomey/Fon religion.

### Research boundary
For DOL research purposes, **Fon and Ewe are separate research tracks with areas of overlap that must be demonstrated through sources rather than assumed.**

---

## 2. Worldview & Religious Concepts — Research Targets

Research should investigate, with community and scholarly context:

- supreme or creator concepts and their different names/understandings
- vodun/vodu as a religious category and its local meanings
- divine, spiritual, ancestral, and other supernatural categories
- relationships between humans, spirits, ancestors, land, water, and community
- moral, social, political, and ritual dimensions of religious life
- sacred places and objects
- divination, healing, priestly/religious specialists, and ritual authority
- oral transmission, songs, stories, praise, and performance
- historical changes caused by migration, kingdoms, Christianity, Islam, colonialism, and the African diaspora

**Important:** Do not assume that a category such as “god,” “spirit,” or “deity” maps cleanly onto every local term.

---

## 3. Figures & Beings — Research Targets

Names frequently encountered in scholarship and public discussion should be researched individually and contextually rather than immediately turned into DOL characters.

Potential research subjects include:

- **Mawu-Lisa** — investigate regional traditions, historical sources, gender/duality interpretations, and variations in the tradition.
- **Sakpata/Sakpatá** — investigate its religious significance, associations, names, regional variations, and historical interpretation.
- **Dan/Dangbé and related serpent imagery** — investigate carefully across Fon/Dahomean and neighboring traditions rather than assuming one universal serpent deity.
- **Aziza and forest-spirit traditions** — research their role, regional variation, and relationship to knowledge/forest traditions.
- other vodun/vodu categories and named beings documented in specific communities.

No figure on this list is approved as a DOL hero, villain, boss, ability, or collectible.

---

## 4. Living Religion & Representation

Vodun/Vodu is not merely a historical mythology or fantasy creature catalogue. Related traditions remain living religious systems.

DOL research must therefore:

- distinguish contemporary practice from historical records
- avoid presenting sacred beings as monsters simply because they oppose the player
- avoid turning ritual practices into generic magic systems without context
- avoid treating sacred objects as ordinary loot
- preserve distinctions between Fon, Ewe, and neighboring traditions
- record regional/community variation
- identify when terminology comes from colonial-era scholarship
- consult contemporary scholars, cultural institutions, and community voices where possible

---

## 5. Evidence & Source Strategy

### Priority sources

1. Academic books and university research
2. Museum, archive, and library collections
3. Historical/ethnographic primary sources, read critically
4. Contemporary scholars and cultural institutions
5. Carefully evaluated secondary material

### Starting scholarly references

- Melville J. Herskovits, *Dahomey: An Ancient West African Kingdom* (1938) — useful historical/ethnographic source, but must be read critically in its colonial-era context.
- Suzanne Preston Blier, *African Vodun: Art, Psychology, and Power* (1995) — important research direction for visual culture, religious concepts, and Dahomean/Fon-related traditions.

These are starting points, not a substitute for broader source verification.

## 6. DOL Classification

Every researched element should eventually receive one of:

- **DOCUMENTED** — supported by reliable evidence.
- **INTERPRETATION** — scholarly interpretation or contested reading.
- **DOL ORIGINAL** — invented specifically for Dawn of Longlat.
- **UNVERIFIED** — encountered but not yet sufficiently supported.

## 7. Adaptation Gate

Before any Fon/Ewe element enters DOL gameplay:

**Source → Context → Variation → Classification → Adaptation proposal → Review → Gameplay**

The adaptation record should answer:

- What source supports this?
- Which community/tradition does it belong to?
- Is it historical, contemporary, or both?
- What variations exist?
- What are we changing?
- Why are we changing it?
- Does the adaptation imply a false historical claim?
- Could the element remain non-playable lore instead?
- Are sacred dimensions being trivialized?
- Is a DOL-original character a better solution?

---

## 8. Open Research Questions

1. Which Fon and Ewe sources provide the strongest community-specific accounts?
2. Which terms are local/self-designations versus labels imposed or standardized by outsiders?
3. How do regional traditions differ across Benin, Togo, and Ghana?
4. Which stories have strong documentation and which are primarily later retellings?
5. How should living religious traditions be represented without converting them into generic fantasy?
6. Which visual/material traditions can inform architecture, clothing, objects, and environments without copying sacred forms?
7. Which elements should remain reference/lore only?
8. Which DOL characters should be entirely original while still drawing on researched concepts?

---

## 9. DOL Rule

> **Fon and Ewe traditions are sources of research, not a pre-made DOL faction.**

DOL may eventually create fictional peoples, characters, realms, creatures, symbols, and conflicts inspired by carefully researched material, but the game must remain clear about what is documented tradition and what is original fiction.

**Research first. Fiction second. Gameplay third.**
