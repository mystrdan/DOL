# Yoruba Traditions — Research Record (Preliminary)

> **Status:** Research draft — not DOL canon  
> **Principle:** Research first. Fiction second.

## 1. Scope

Yoruba traditions encompass religious, philosophical, oral, artistic, and social traditions associated with Yoruba-speaking peoples, principally in present-day Nigeria and Benin, with important historical and contemporary diaspora communities.

**DOL rule:** “Yoruba mythology” is not treated as a single frozen body of stories. Regional, historical, lineage, and contemporary differences must be preserved.

## 2. Core Research Areas

### Olodumare / Olorun

Yoruba religious thought includes concepts and names associated with a supreme creator, commonly represented in scholarship through forms including **Olodumare** and **Olorun**.

**DOL status:** Research only. Do not reduce Yoruba theology to a generic fantasy “god.”

### Orisa

**Orisa** are central to Yoruba religious traditions. Individual Orisa have distinct histories, associations, praise traditions, stories, symbols, and relationships.

Important research subjects include figures such as:

- Obatala
- Oduduwa
- Ogun
- Sango
- Yemoja
- Esu
- Oshun

**DOL status:** Each figure requires its own research record. The list above is a research queue, not a DOL character roster.

### Esu

Esu is a particularly important research case because interpretations and translations have varied substantially, including historical attempts to equate Esu with Christian/European concepts that can distort Yoruba understandings.

**DOL rule:** Never automatically label Esu as “the devil.”

### Ifa

**Ifa** is a major Yoruba divinatory and knowledge tradition associated with Orunmila and a large corpus of verses and teachings.

**DOL status:** Research only. Do not turn Ifa into a generic spellbook or random “magic system.”

### Ancestors and the Dead

Yoruba traditions contain complex relationships among the living, ancestors, the dead, and spiritual forces.

**DOL status:** Requires context-specific research. Avoid reducing these relationships to generic undead enemies.

## 3. Storytelling & Oral Tradition

Research should include:

- Orisa stories
- praise poetry
- Ifa verses
- oral histories
- proverbs
- creation narratives
- heroic traditions
- trickster narratives
- regional variations

Oral traditions should be treated as living bodies of knowledge rather than merely fantasy plot material.

## 4. Symbols, Art & Material Culture

Research separately:

- crowns and regalia
- ritual objects
- sacred spaces
- sculptures
- masks
- textiles
- beadwork
- architecture
- drums and musical traditions
- visual symbolism

**DOL rule:** Visual inspiration must retain context. Do not use recognizable sacred or ceremonial objects as meaningless fantasy decoration.

## 5. Source Strategy

Prioritize:

1. Yoruba/community and cultural-institution sources.
2. Academic research and university publications.
3. Museums, archives, libraries, and established cultural institutions.
4. Primary or early documentary sources, read critically.
5. Carefully evaluated secondary scholarship.

Early colonial/missionary descriptions may contain useful historical evidence but must be compared with later scholarship and Yoruba perspectives.

## 6. DOL Adaptation Rules

- Yoruba traditions remain distinct from Akan and every other researched tradition.
- Do not merge Orisa into a generic “African pantheon.”
- Do not make a real-world sacred figure evil simply because the game needs an antagonist.
- Do not equate Yoruba spiritual concepts with European fantasy categories without explanation.
- A DOL-original character may draw inspiration from documented narrative patterns while remaining clearly fictional.
- Any direct use of a named traditional figure requires a dedicated adaptation review.
- Ifa material requires exceptional care because it is an extensive knowledge/divination tradition, not simply a collection of magical powers.

## 7. Research Questions

- How do major scholarly sources describe Olodumare/Olorun and their relationship to Orisa?
- How do different Yoruba communities describe individual Orisa?
- Which stories have strong historical documentation?
- Which narratives vary substantially by region or lineage?
- What is the appropriate context for praise poetry and Ifa verses?
- Which visual/material elements are appropriate for fictional inspiration?
- Which elements should remain outside DOL gameplay?
- How can Yoruba scholars and cultural institutions be represented in the research process?
