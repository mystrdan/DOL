# Akan Traditions — Research Record (Preliminary)

> **Status:** Research draft — not DOL canon  
> **Principle:** Research first. Fiction second.

## 1. Scope

“Akan” refers to a broad cultural-linguistic grouping spanning parts of present-day Ghana and Côte d’Ivoire. It contains multiple communities and traditions, including Asante/Ashanti, Fante, Akuapem, Akyem and others.

**DOL rule:** Akan traditions must never be treated as one perfectly uniform mythology. Where a belief, name, symbol, or story belongs specifically to one community, DOL will preserve that attribution.

## 2. Research Questions

Before adapting Akan material into DOL, establish:

- Which community/tradition does the material come from?
- What language or historical spelling is being used?
- Is the source describing a living tradition, an older ethnographic account, or a modern interpretation?
- What evidence supports the claim?
- Is the element public cultural knowledge, context-dependent, or sacred/restricted?
- What changes would DOL make, and why?

## 3. High-Level Research Areas

### Supreme/Creator Concepts

Names and descriptions associated with a supreme creator occur across Akan traditions, including forms such as **Nyame** and **Onyankopon**. These terms and their theological interpretations require community- and source-specific treatment.

**DOL status:** Research only. Do not turn this into a simplified “one African god” mechanic.

### Spiritual Beings

Akan religious traditions include categories of spiritual beings often discussed in English-language scholarship as deities, divinities, or spirits. **Abosom** is a key term requiring contextual study.

**DOL status:** Research only. Record individual beings with their specific cultural context rather than creating a generic “Akan pantheon.”

### Ancestors

Ancestors are an important area of Akan religious and social thought. Their role, terminology, rites, and relationship to the living require subgroup-specific research.

**DOL status:** Research only. Do not reduce ancestors to generic fantasy ghosts.

### Ananse

**Ananse**, the spider/trickster figure, is one of the best-known Akan storytelling figures and has a major history in oral tradition and the African diaspora.

**DOL status:** Potential inspiration. Any direct adaptation must distinguish documented storytelling material from a DOL-original character.

### Adinkra

Adinkra symbols are associated particularly with Akan/Asante cultural history and communicate proverbial, philosophical, social, and other meanings.

**DOL status:** Separate symbol research required. Do not assume every Adinkra symbol is a religious symbol, and do not assign meanings without a source.

### Asante Golden Stool

The Golden Stool is specifically associated with Asante history and political/spiritual identity.

**DOL status:** Research separately as Asante material. It must not be presented as a generic “Akan artifact.”

## 4. Source Priority

The following are starting points for verification, not substitutes for source review:

1. R. S. Rattray — *Ashanti* (1923).
2. R. S. Rattray — *Religion and Art in Ashanti* (1927).
3. Kofi Asare Opoku — *West African Traditional Religion* (1978).
4. Kwame Gyekye — *An Essay on African Philosophical Thought: The Akan Conceptual Scheme* (1987).

Colonial-era ethnographies such as Rattray should be read critically and compared with later scholarship and, where possible, community and cultural-institution sources.

## 5. Adaptation Rules for DOL

- Real Akan traditions remain distinct from the fictional Longlat world.
- Do not make a documented sacred figure evil merely to create an antagonist.
- Do not merge Akan figures with Yoruba, Fon/Ewe, Mande, Egyptian, Kongo, Maasai, Zulu, Xhosa, San, or other traditions for convenience.
- A DOL-original character may be inspired by a documented archetype or story pattern, but the adaptation must be explicitly labelled.
- Cultural symbols should not be used as meaningless decoration.
- Gameplay mechanics must not imply historical claims that the source tradition itself does not make.

## 6. DOL Adaptation Pipeline

**Documented tradition → Context → Research record → Interpretation → DOL-original adaptation → Gameplay**

No gameplay mechanic becomes canon until its research status is recorded.

## 7. Open Research

- Map major Akan subgroups separately.
- Verify terminology and spelling across sources.
- Research Ananse stories by source and community context.
- Research Asante religious/political history separately from broader Akan material.
- Research Adinkra symbols individually rather than as a single category.
- Document variations between historical sources and contemporary scholarship.
- Identify Ghanaian cultural institutions and scholars whose material can strengthen the research layer.
- Record page-level references for every claim that eventually enters the game bible.
